⑤ The last gate — the production form

zenith.studio · 2026-09-02

Once you have 12 testers for 14 days you can apply for production. The button does not take you straight to review — a form opens first. Three steps, nine questions, every free-text box capped at exactly 300 characters, two multiple choice. Here is every question, so you can draft answers before you open it. (Translated from the Korean form; Google's English wording may differ slightly.) [Step 1 - Closed testing] 1. How did you recruit the users who took part in your closed test? For example, did you ask friends or family? Or did you use a paid testing service? 2. How easy was it to recruit testers for your app? (very hard ~ very easy) 3. Describe how engaged your testers were during the closed test. Tell us whether they used all of the app's features, and whether the way they used it matches how you expect real users to use it. If it does not match, explain what differences you expect. 4. Summarise the feedback you received from testers. Also tell us how you collected it. [Step 2 - App info] 5. Who is the main audience for your app? 6. Describe the value your app provides to users. 7. How many installs do you expect in the first year? (0-10K / 10K-100K / 100K-1M / 1M+ / don't know) [Step 3 - Production readiness] 8. How did you change the app based on what you learned in the closed test? 9. How did you decide the app is ready for production?

Questions 4 and 8 are where it is decided. Two weeks later you try to remember what anyone actually said and you cannot, so you write something that sounds right — and the reviewer reads a few hundred of those a day. The answer is pulled out of what you kept during the 14 days. Reply to feedback as it arrives; your reply is the record of what you did about it. The History tab groups it by version and exports it as one page. For question 3, if you found testers by trading, honesty reads better — developers tested each other, and here is how that differs from real use. One more thing: your answers are not shown on your Play listing. The form says so on screen.

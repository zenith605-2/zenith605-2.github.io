What Google actually checks

Updated August 2026

If your personal developer account was created after 13 November 2023, you cannot publish to production until you have run a closed test. The rule, in Google's own words, is this:

At least 12 testers must be opted in to your closed test when you apply for production access, and they must have been opted in continuously for the preceding 14 days.

It counts opt-in time, not opens

This is the part most people get wrong. The requirement is about how long someone stayed opted in. It is not a count of how many times they launched your app.

Play Console words it as "testers who opted in, for N days". If a tester joins your Google Group, taps "Become a tester", and then never installs anything, they still count toward the twelve. Developers report passing with testers who were on iPhones and never installed at all.

Uninstalling does not remove someone from your tester list either. Once the 14 days are behind you, a tester deleting the app does not undo it.

So why does everyone say testers must be active?

Two reasons, and only one of them is Google.

1. Continuity, not activity

The 14 days must be continuous. If someone opts out — or you remove them from the group — the clock for that tester resets. That is what breaks tests, not a quiet day.

2. The application form asks you to explain yourself

After you meet the bar, you apply for production access and answer questions in three sections:

SectionWhat it wants
About your closed testHow you ran it, who tested, what you learned
About your app/gameWhat it does, who it is for
About your production readinessWhether it is finished enough to ship

There is no field that requires you to attach tester feedback. But a test where nobody opened anything gives you nothing to write in section one, and reviewers do read it.

What this means for you

  • Do not panic over a skipped day. A tester who missed Tuesday has not broken anything.
  • Do panic over someone leaving the group. That resets their continuity.
  • Keep the test open to all countries unless you have a reason not to. "Not available in my country" is one of the most common ways a tester silently drops out.
  • You still want real use. Not because Google counts it, but because you need something honest to write in the form — and because the person who traded with you asked you to test their app, not to sit in a list.

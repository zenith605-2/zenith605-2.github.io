Why testers disappear

Four failure modes, in the order they bite

1. They cannot install it at all

Your closed test is limited to a few countries and the tester is not in one of them. They tap the link, get "not available in your country", and say nothing — you just watch the counter stall.

Fix: open the test to all countries unless you have a real reason not to. This is the single most common silent drop-off, and it costs you nothing to prevent.

2. The group link is broken and you cannot see it

If your Google Group's Who can view conversations is set to "Group members", anyone who is not already a member hits a permission wall. You will never see it yourself — you are the owner, so it looks fine from your side.

Fix: open your group settings and set both "Who can see group" and "Who can join" to Anyone on the web. Then check the link in a private window.

3. They install, then quietly uninstall

Phone storage, a cleanup, or they simply lost interest. The tester list still shows them, so nothing looks wrong. Google still counts them as opted in — but they are not testing, and if you are trading, you are the one holding up your end alone.

Fix: you cannot prevent it, but you can see it. ACT Party marks a trade when the app is no longer on the phone, so the other side is not left guessing.

4. The slow fade

The most common one. Nothing goes wrong; they just stop opening it. Day three is fine, day six is fine, day nine is a maybe. Across 21 finished trades we measured, the average was 10.3 opens out of 14 days and nobody hit all 14.

Fix: reciprocity, not nagging. When someone can see that you opened their app today, they open yours. That loop works better than a reminder, because a reminder from someone who is not doing their own half is just annoying.

What none of this is

It is not usually bad faith. Almost every drop-off above is a person who meant to help and hit friction. Design for friction, not for cheating.

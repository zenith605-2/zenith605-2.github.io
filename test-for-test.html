Test for test: filling 12 closed testers by swapping with other developers

Google Play closed testing, 12 testers for 14 days — done by developers testing each other's apps

A new personal developer account cannot publish straight to Google Play. First 12 testers have to stay opted in to a closed test for 14 days, and only then does the production application open. A test swap (often posted as "test for test" or "T4T") fills those 12 by trading: you become a tester of someone's app, and they become a tester of yours. They need 12 testers too, so nobody is asking a favour.

Check your own three links before you look for anyone

If your partner cannot install your app, the swap never starts. Before you post, open these three with a Google account that is not yours.

  1. Google Group — a new group is private by default. Unless you make it public, your partner sees "Content unavailable". → Google Group setup
  2. Opt-in link — signed in with the account that joined the group, it should show "Become a tester". It will not open until a release on the track has passed review.
  3. Countries — if the partner's country is not on your closed test, they get "not available in your country". Swapping across countries means opening the test widely. → Country settings

An internal testing link does not count

Play Console has several test tracks. Google's help page says at least 12 testers must opt in to a closed test. Hand out an internal testing link and your partner can install and open the app every day without being counted.

Where to find swap partners

  • A swap board app — apps that need testers are listed in one place; you list yours and send requests. No posts to write. → Apps listed on ACT Party
  • Developer communities — subreddits and forums where people post "test for test". Put the name of their app in your first message so it is clear you read their post.
  • People you know — fill whatever friends cannot cover with swaps.

Knowing they installed it, and are still opening it

The usual swap argument is "did you really install it?" and "are you still in?". Trading screenshots proves one moment on day one; the other 13 days run on trust. On ACT Party the days each side opened the other's app are read from Android usage stats and recorded automatically, so both of you see the same record with no screenshots. Never trade reviews or ratings — exchanging reviews breaks Google Play policy.

Line up the end date

If a partner leaves the test before day 14, they stop counting toward your 12. Agree at the start that both of you stay until both periods end. Landing on exactly 12 means one dropout puts you back to waiting, so gather a few more. → Why testers disappear

Testers do not have to open the app on all 14 days; what Google looks at is that the opt-in lasted 14 days. → What Google actually checks · How often testers really open the app

After the 12

12 testers for 14 days unlocks the production application. The form then asks how testers used your app and what feedback they gave, so keep what your swap partners told you. → The production access form

FAQ

Do internal testing testers count toward the 12?
No. Only people opted in to a closed test count.
What happens if my swap partner leaves before 14 days?
They stop counting. Agree on the end date up front and gather a few extra.
Can we swap reviews or ratings too?
No. That breaks Google Play policy.
Does reaching 12 testers guarantee production access?
No. It only unlocks the application; review is separate. Partner feedback helps with the form.
